# Final Task – Cyber Security Policy Presentation

**Business:** *Café Lumière* – a small local café with an online ordering website

## PART 1 – Cyber Security Policy

### Slide 1 – Title
**On the slide:**
- *Café Lumière – Cyber Security Policy & Emergency Plan*
- Names of the group members

**🎤 Speaker A:**
> Good morning everyone. We are the team behind Café Lumière, a small local café with an online ordering website. Today we are going to present our cyber security policy, our emergency management plan, and our approach to staff training. I'm [name], the IT and security manager, and I'm here with [name], the café owner, and [name], our staff trainer.

---

### Slide 2 – Our Business and Our Risks
**On the slide:**
- 8 employees, 2 tills, 3 laptops, 1 tablet for orders
- Online ordering website with customer accounts and card payments
- Main risks: phishing scams, payment fraud, ransomware, lost or stolen devices

**🎤 Speaker B:**
> Thank you. So, let me introduce our café. We have eight employees, two tills, three laptops and one tablet for taking orders. Most importantly, we have an online ordering website, which means we store customer names, addresses and payment details. That makes us a target. The main risks for us are phishing scams, online payment fraud, ransomware and lost or stolen devices. That's why we decided we needed a clear policy.

---

### Slide 3 – Strong Passwords & Multi-Factor Authentication
**On the slide:**
- Passphrases of at least 14 characters
- A different password for every account
- A password manager for all staff
- Multi-factor authentication (MFA) on email, website admin and banking

**🎤 Speaker C:**
> Our first measure is about passwords, and we enforce this policy strictly. Every employee must use a long passphrase of at least fourteen characters, and never reuse the same password twice. To make it easier, we give everyone a password manager. On top of that, we turn on multi-factor authentication for email, the website admin panel and online banking. So even if a password is stolen, an attacker can't log in without the second code.

---

### Slide 4 – Access Controls
**On the slide:**
- Principle of "least privilege": staff only access what they need
- Only the owner and the IT manager have admin rights
- Accounts closed the same day an employee leaves
- Access reviewed every 3 months

**🎤 Speaker A:**
> Next, access controls. We don't give all staff full access to all systems – that would be a big mistake. A waiter only needs the till and the order tablet, not the accounting files. Only the owner and I have administrator rights. When someone leaves the company, their accounts are closed the same day, and we review who has access to what every three months.

---

### Slide 5 – Software Updates & Malware Protection
**On the slide:**
- Automatic updates turned on for all devices
- Updates installed within 48 hours
- Antivirus and a malware **filter** on email
- Physical security: devices locked, never left unattended

**🎤 Speaker B:**
> As the owner, I want our devices to be safe and up to date. So we turn on automatic updates on every device, and any important update must be installed within forty-eight hours. We also use antivirus software and a malware filter on our email to block dangerous files. And don't forget physical security: laptops are locked away at night and the order tablet is never left unattended, because a stolen device can give a criminal access to our data.

---

### Slide 6 – Backups
**On the slide:**
- Automatic daily **backup** of orders, customer data and accounts
- One copy in the cloud, one copy offline
- Restoring a backup is tested every month

**🎤 Speaker C:**
> Our next point is backups, which small businesses often ignore. Every day, our orders, customer data and accounting files are automatically backed up. We keep one copy in the cloud and one copy offline, disconnected from the network, so ransomware can't reach it. And every month we test that we can really restore the data – a backup you can't restore is useless.

---

### Slide 7 – Protecting Customer Information
**On the slide:**
- Collect only the data we need
- Card payments handled by a secure payment provider – we never store card numbers
- Website protected with HTTPS
- Legal **compliance** with privacy laws

**🎤 Speaker A:**
> Customer privacy is essential for us. First, we only collect the data we really need. Second, card payments go through a secure payment provider, so we never store card numbers ourselves – this greatly reduces the risk of payment fraud. Our website is protected with HTTPS. All of this ensures our legal compliance with privacy laws and keeps the trust of our customers.

---

### Slide 8 – Incident Reporting
**On the slide:**
- "If in doubt, report it!"
- Report any suspicious email, call or device problem to the IT manager immediately
- No blame for reporting a mistake
- Every **incident** recorded in an incident log

**🎤 Speaker B:**
> Our rule for incident reporting is simple: "If in doubt, report it!" If an employee receives a suspicious email, a strange phone call from someone pretending to be from the government or the bank, or notices a problem on a device, they must report it to the IT manager immediately. And I want to insist on this: nobody will be blamed for reporting a mistake. It's much worse to hide it. Every incident is written down in an incident log.

## PART 2 – One-Page Emergency Management Plan

### Slide 9 – Incident Response: 6 Steps
**On the slide:**
1. **Detect** – recognise the signs of an attack
2. **Isolate** – disconnect affected devices from the network
3. **Report** – alert the manager and the IT team; preserve evidence
4. **Assess** – identify which systems and data are affected
5. **Recover** – restore clean backups, change all passwords
6. **Review** – learn lessons and update the policy

**🎤 Speaker A:**
> Now let's move on to our emergency management plan. Imagine we suffer a ransomware attack. Step one: detect – staff recognise the warning signs, like files that won't open or a ransom message. Step two: isolate – we immediately disconnect the affected devices from the network so the attack can't spread. Step three: report – we alert the manager and the IT team, and we preserve the evidence. We never pay the ransom.

**🎤 Speaker C:**
> Thank you, [name]. Step four: assess – we identify which systems and which customer data are affected. Step five: recover – we restore our clean offline backup and change all the passwords. And finally, step six: review – once everything is working again, we analyse how the attack happened and we update our policy and our training.

---

### Slide 10 – Communication During a Crisis
**On the slide:**
- **Staff:** informed by the manager by phone / group message (not company email)
- **Customers:** informed within 72 hours if their data is affected
- **Authorities:** report to the national cyber security agency and the police in case of fraud
- **Partners:** contact the bank, the payment provider and the insurer
- Cyber **liability** insurance covers recovery and legal costs

**🎤 Speaker B:**
> Communication is a key part of the plan, and that's my responsibility as the owner. First, I inform the staff by phone or group message, not by company email, because it may be compromised. If customer data is affected, we inform our customers within seventy-two hours and explain what they should do. We also report the attack to the national cyber security agency and to the police in case of fraud. Finally, I contact our bank, our payment provider and our insurer. That's why we have cyber liability insurance: it helps cover recovery costs and legal expenses.

---

## PART 3 – Staff Training & Customer Data Protection

### Slide 11 – Ongoing Staff Training
**On the slide:**
- Mandatory training for **all** employees, from day one
- Short 20-minute session every 3 months
- Fake phishing emails to test staff
- Monthly "scam alert" on the staff noticeboard

**🎤 Speaker C:**
> As the staff trainer, I believe that our employees are our first line of defence. So cyber security training is mandatory for everyone, whatever their role – a waiter can be targeted just like a manager. New employees are trained on their first day, and then we have a short twenty-minute session every three months. We also send fake phishing emails to test everyone, and each month we put a "scam alert" on the staff noticeboard to keep everyone updated about new threats.

---

### Slide 12 – Conclusion: Why Vote for Café Lumière?
**On the slide:**
- Strong passwords + MFA + limited access
- Tested backups and a clear 6-step emergency plan
- Trained and alert staff
- Customer data protected = customer trust
- **Our goal: cyber resilience**

**🎤 Speaker B:**
> To conclude, we think Café Lumière is well prepared. Our policy is simple enough for a small team, but strong enough to protect us.

**🎤 Speaker C:**
> Our staff are trained, alert, and they know exactly what to do and who to call if something goes wrong.

**🎤 Speaker A:**
> And with tested backups and a clear emergency plan, we can recover quickly from an attack. That's what we call cyber resilience. So we hope you'll vote for Café Lumière as the business most likely to avoid a cyber incident! Thank you for listening – do you have any questions?
